WHERE I'M STARTING

Google review replies, written in your voice.

One service, done properly, before I add anything else.

Google's local search results favor businesses that answer their reviews. Most independent contractors know this and still don't do it — not because they don't care, but because the reviews come in while they're on a roof. So the reviews sit there unanswered, and a competitor who replies to every one of them ranks above you.

It sounds like you

I tune the replies to how you actually talk — not corporate filler. Short, specific, and different every time. No template a customer can spot.

Good reviews get handled

Four and five stars are most of your volume and almost no risk. Those move fast, so someone who took the time to praise you hears back while it still matters.

Bad reviews come to you

One to three stars are never posted without you. You get a calm draft that invites the customer to call you directly — then you approve it, rewrite it, or handle it yourself.

THE PART THAT MATTERS

An angry customer is not a job for a robot.

This is the one design decision I'd defend hardest. A tone-deaf automatic reply to a furious customer is worse than silence — it's public, it's permanent, and it's your name on it. So the system splits by rating, and the tough ones stop at your desk.

4–5 ★  ·  HANDS OFF

Handled for you

A warm, specific thank-you in your voice. You approve the approach once during setup and then stop thinking about it.

1–3 ★  ·  HELD FOR YOU

Your call, every time

Drafted and sent to you — never published on its own. Most owners want to handle these personally, and they should.

HOW SETUP GOES

Four steps, and you're out of it.

  1. We talk for fifteen minutes

    I look at your profile beforehand. If replies aren't actually your problem, I'll tell you that instead of selling you something.

  2. I learn your voice

    A short call or a few of your own past replies is enough. You see sample replies and tell me what's off before anything goes live.

  3. You sign off on the rules

    In writing: what gets handled automatically, what always comes to you, and what I'm allowed to do on your profile. Google requires this and so do I.

  4. It runs, and I watch it

    You get billed once it's confirmed working on your actual profile — not before. If you want out, I hand your profile access back and we're done.

STRAIGHT ANSWERS

What this is, and what it isn't.

I write replies. I do not write reviews.

Not ever, not for a fee, not "just to get started." Fake reviews violate Google's policies and federal law, and they get businesses delisted. If that's what you're shopping for, I'm the wrong call.
